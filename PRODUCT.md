# Product

## Register

product

## Users

ContextRail serves three overlapping groups:

- Managers and service operators who describe an outcome in plain language and need the system to finish the work without bypassing policy.
- Human approvers who need a compact, evidence-backed reason to approve or reject a privileged action.
- Platform teams who need reusable MCP tools, portable agent skills, deterministic audit history, and clear failure modes across Freshworks and external systems.

For the hackathon, the immediate audience is a technical judging panel evaluating whether the product is original, credible, useful, and demonstrably agentic within a two-minute walkthrough.

## Product Purpose

ContextRail turns scattered enterprise knowledge into governed, verified action. It compiles a request, subject record, evidence, policy decisions, plan, handoff history, blockers, approvals, and execution results into one portable Context Capsule that survives every specialist-agent handoff.

Success means a reviewer can answer four questions without reconstructing the workflow: what was requested, why each decision was made, who or what acted, and whether the result was verified. The product must make refusals as legible and valuable as successful actions.

## Brand Personality

Operational, evidentiary, calm.

The product should feel like a trusted control room: dense where expertise requires density, restrained where attention is scarce, and explicit whenever authority or risk changes hands.

## Anti-references

- Generic “AI agent” dashboards with neon gradients, glowing glass cards, chat bubbles, robot imagery, or decorative network diagrams.
- Template-heavy SaaS layouts made from repeated cards, oversized hero metrics, and tiny uppercase labels on every section.
- Security theatre: green checkmarks without evidence, approval prompts without policy context, or audit trails that merely restate “success.”
- Vendor-assistant branding or generated-by metadata. ContextRail must read as a product, not as authoring-tool output.
- Friendly-but-vague copy that hides denials, retries, partial completion, or stale evidence.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the decision, not the claim.** Every important status must reveal its evidence, policy clause, approver, or verification probe.
2. **Make the capsule the protagonist.** Screens should show one request gaining context and control as it moves through the rail, rather than presenting disconnected feature panels.
3. **Refusal is a successful outcome.** A denied unsafe action remains visible, cited, and terminal; it is never quietly dropped from the plan.
4. **Progressive disclosure over dashboard sprawl.** Lead with the outcome and current blocker, then let technical detail unfold in a predictable order.
5. **One signalling language.** Green means verified, amber means held, red means stopped, and blue means the capsule in motion. These colors never decorate unrelated content.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. All workflows must be usable with a keyboard, maintain visible focus, expose status without relying on color alone, respect reduced motion, preserve readable text at narrow widths and 200% zoom, and provide at least 44×44 px touch targets on touch-oriented layouts. Dense technical text may be compact on desktop, but mobile body copy must remain at least 14 px.
